Folding

A phone’s footprint. Seven phones thick.

  1. Rear arms first. They swing forward and tuck under the body.
  2. Front arms over the top. They swing back and lie along the sides.
  3. 12.5 × 8 × 5.5 cm. Jacket pocket, yes. Jeans pocket, no.

Unfolding is the same in reverse: front arms out first, then rear.

Under the shell

Eight parts. All of them replaceable.

From published teardowns of this frame. Exact chips change between factory batches.

  • Top shell: snaps off.
  • 6-axis gyro and accelerometer: keeps it level. This is what you calibrate.
  • Barometer: holds height when you center the left stick.
  • Flight controller: a small 32-bit chip turns your stick moves into four motor speeds.
  • Wi-Fi module: streams the camera to your phone.
  • Camera: tilts 90 degrees from the remote.
  • Four coreless brushed motors: light, cheap, sold as spares.
  • 3.7 V LiPo battery: slides out of the back.

      Questions

      Asked before buying.

      Can a kid fly it?

      Sellers rate it 14 and up. Younger pilots do fine with an adult next to them, prop guards on and speed set to 1. Small parts make it unsuitable for under-3s.

      Can I fly it indoors?

      In a big room with the guards on, yes. It has no position hold, so it wanders. Clear the space and keep it away from faces and ceiling fans.

      How windy is too windy?

      If leaves are moving, wait. It weighs about 100 g and has no GPS, so a breeze will carry it off faster than you can fly it back.

      Does it have GPS or return-to-home?

      No GPS. The one-key return flies it back along the direction it took off in. It gets it close; you bring it the rest of the way.

      What app does it use, and where do videos go?

      A free Wi-Fi app, named on the box and in the manual’s QR code. Your phone joins the drone’s Wi-Fi, shows the live view, and saves photos and video to the phone.

      What breaks first, and can I fix it?

      Propellers, almost always. Four spares come in the box. Arms, motors and props for this frame are sold widely and cheaply, and a small screwdriver is all it takes.

      How do I look after the battery?

      It’s a LiPo. Charge it where you can see it, let it cool after a flight before charging, and store it about half full. If a pack puffs up, stop using it and recycle it.

      Does obstacle avoidance work?

      Some batches have it and some don’t, and owners say it isn’t reliable. Fly as if it isn’t there.
